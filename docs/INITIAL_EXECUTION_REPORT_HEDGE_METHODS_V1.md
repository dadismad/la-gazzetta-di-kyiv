# Initial Execution Report — Hedge-Fund Methodologies for Demand/Supply Decomposition (V1)

## A) Executive synthesis
1. The prevailing edge is no longer pure sentiment; it is **multi-layer fusion**: sentiment + positioning + liquidity + options.
2. Sophisticated desks model **attention as a state variable**, not a standalone alpha.
3. Demand inference is strongest when narrative acceleration aligns with flow proxies (ETF flows, options call skew, dispersion collapse).
4. Supply inference improves with dealer gamma context, inventory pressure, and macro hedging constraints.
5. The most robust stacks are regime-aware; fixed coefficients fail during narrative regime flips.
6. Entity-level graphing (company, policymaker, commodity node) outperforms keyword-level sentiment.
7. Contradiction detection (consensus claim vs market behavior) is a practical source of short-horizon alpha.
8. Invalidation discipline is central: time-stop, flow-stop, and volatility-stop conditions.
9. Editorial outputs should mirror buy-side structure: thesis -> mechanism -> probability -> invalidation.
10. Gazzetta advantage: convert complex decomposition into high-clarity narrative products at higher cadence.

## B) Methodology map (ranked)
1. **Regime-switching multi-factor nowcasting**
   - Inputs: returns, volume, vol, macro surprises, sentiment/attention indices.
   - Strength: adaptive to non-stationarity.
   - Failure mode: unstable transition probabilities in shock windows.

2. **NLP + entity graph narrative models**
   - Inputs: news/social text, entity linking, relationship graph.
   - Strength: identifies who captures recognition spillover.
   - Failure mode: sarcasm/irony/domain drift.

3. **Options-implied positioning models**
   - Inputs: skew term-structure, gamma exposure proxies, OI changes.
   - Strength: reveals hidden demand/supply and hedging reflexivity.
   - Failure mode: poor signal in low-liquidity tails.

4. **Cross-asset transmission models**
   - Inputs: DXY/rates/credit/equity/commodities/crypto co-moves.
   - Strength: finds second-order effects.
   - Failure mode: correlation breakdown during policy shocks.

5. **Event-driven Bayesian update engines**
   - Inputs: event priors, realized outcomes, narrative responses.
   - Strength: explicit probability updates.
   - Failure mode: poor priors under novel regimes.

## C) Demand-side decomposition
- Demand buckets:
  1) passive allocators (index/benchmark constraints)
  2) systematic trend/momentum
  3) discretionary macro/sector
  4) retail narrative chasers
- Key question per bucket: what narrative converts attention into mandatory/likely buying?
- Metrics:
  - engagement velocity
  - flow proxies
  - breakout + follow-through probability

## D) Supply-side decomposition
- Supply buckets:
  1) long-only rebalance sellers
  2) hedgers/dealers (gamma/vanna pressure)
  3) fundamental shorts
  4) forced liquidity providers
- Supply stress indicators:
  - widening spreads
  - rising implied vol without spot confirmation
  - block trade frequency

## E) Beneficiary radar (current framework)
Under "recognition -> capital" dynamics, beneficiaries often cluster in:
1. AI compute & semis infrastructure
2. defense/dual-use autonomy systems
3. grid/power and electricity-intense infrastructure
4. energy logistics and transition bottlenecks
5. software orchestration layers attached to dominant narratives

## F) Failure modes and anti-overfitting
- Failure modes:
  - attention-chasing without flow confirmation
  - regime inversion after policy intervention
  - narrative spoofing/manipulation bursts
- Controls:
  - require 2-of-3 confirmation (attention, flow, options)
  - impose time-decay on stale narratives
  - maintain explicit invalidation triggers in every output
