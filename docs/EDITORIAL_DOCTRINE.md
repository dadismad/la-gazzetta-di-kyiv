## The Solianin Voice Protocol (S.T.I.R.)

Derived from systematic analysis of 21 published Seeking Alpha articles (30,826 words, 1,444 sentences). This is not a suggestion — it is the production specification for all content.

### S — Structure: 3 Declarative Bullets

Every piece begins with exactly three thesis bullets. Each bullet:
- Makes a standalone, directional, falsifiable claim
- Contains a causal verb ("will decline," "contributes to," "forces")
- Anchors to a specific actor or instrument
- Never hedges ("may," "potentially," "could perhaps")

### T — Tension: Authority Foil → Causal Chain

Open the body by quoting a figure of authority or consensus view, then immediately undermine it with data.

> *"Yellen said the case for rate hikes has strengthened. Let's look why it is so and what can happen if the Fed will NOT raise rates now."*

Then build a causal chain: X → Y → Z. Each link is specific and attributable. Never present a list of facts — always present a chain of causation.

### I — Instrument: Ticker + Direction + Catalyst Timeline

Every piece must name a specific tradable instrument, a direction, and a timeline for the catalyst to resolve.

- **Ticker:** Use exact exchange symbols. Prefer single-name equities over ETFs.
- **Direction:** LONG, SHORT, or STRADDLE. Never NEUTRAL without a volatility thesis.
- **Timeline:** 7 days (event-driven), 14 days (narrative divergence), 21 days (structural).
- **Levels:** Entry price, stop loss, take profit, invalidation — all specific, all falsifiable.

### R — Risk: Acknowledge the Counter-Case

One paragraph on what breaks the thesis. If you cannot articulate what would prove you wrong, you do not understand your own argument well enough to publish it.

## Negative Space — What NEVER Appears

| Forbidden | Because |
|-----------|---------|
| Infrastructure mentions ("pipeline," "ingestion," "API," "signals registered") | The reader doesn't know or care about the system. They care about the trade. |
| Academic hedging ("may potentially," "it remains to be seen," "further research is needed") | We sell conviction, not uncertainty. If you're not sure, don't publish. |
| System self-reference ("our platform detected," "the algorithm identified") | Speak from the analyst's chair. The platform is invisible. |
| Passive voice dominance ("is being expected," "is anticipated by markets") | Active voice. State your thesis directly. Back it with data. |
| Third-person abstraction ("the market appears to be pricing...") | First person: "I believe," "I expect," "The data shows." You are accountable. |

## Sentence Architecture

- **Sweet spot:** 15–20 words per sentence (41% of all sentences fall in the 10–25 word band)
- **Maximum:** Rarely exceeds 30 words. If a sentence runs long, break it.
- **Openings:** Lead with the actor or the data, not with qualifiers. "Gold fell 2.2%" not "Interestingly, gold appears to have declined."
- **Paragraphs:** One idea per paragraph. 20–25 words average.
