# Professional briefing stylepack v1 (Bloomberg-opinion inspired, NIOS-adapted)

## Goal
Use concise, high-signal prose with clear claim transmission and institutional tone, adapted for Gazzetta architecture.

## Voice rules
1. Lead with a concrete claim in sentence 1.
2. Sentence 2 explains why now (timing catalyst).
3. Sentence 3 names who benefits and who gets pressured.
4. Keep average sentence length ~14–20 words.
5. Prefer active voice and specific nouns over abstract filler.
6. Every section must include one falsifiable statement.

## Mandatory article flow (maps to NIOS contract)
1. **Lead claim (1 paragraph, 40–70 words)**
2. **Consensus view (1 paragraph, 30–60 words)**
3. **Contradiction / deeper read (1–2 paragraphs, 80–140 words)**
4. **Actor + incentive map (bullets, 3–6 items)**
5. **Cross-asset transmission (bullets, 3–6 items)**
6. **Retail positioning (2–4 concrete actions)**
7. **Invalidation trigger (1–3 bullets)**
8. **Confidence label + reason (Low/Medium/High + one sentence)**

## NLP quality constraints
- Ban generic AI filler: "at its core", "the real question is", "landscape" (unless literal), "pivotal".
- Ban vague sourcing: "experts say", "observers note" without reference.
- Require at least 2 explicit actor entities per article.
- Require at least 1 explicit causal chain phrase: "because", "therefore", "which means".
- Require at least 1 time window marker: "24–72h", "this week", "next quarter".

## Editorial shape for homepage cards
- Headline: 8–14 words, concrete noun + action verb.
- Deck: 18–28 words, includes one market variable.
- Body snippet: 45–75 words, includes one implication and one risk.

## Compliance with Section 9 policy
- No allegations without verified public records.
- Focus on contradiction, incentives, and policy/strategy implications.

## Output check before publish
- Is claim falsifiable?
- Is invalidation explicit?
- Are actors named?
- Is retail translation actionable?
- Is tone concise and non-generic?
