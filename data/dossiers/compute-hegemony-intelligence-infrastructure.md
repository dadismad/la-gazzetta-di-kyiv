## Structural Thesis

Artificial intelligence is not a software story — it is a silicon supply chain story. The geopolitical control of advanced semiconductor fabrication has become the single most important structural determinant of national security, economic competitiveness, and military capability in the 21st century.

The thesis has three layers. First, the hardware bottleneck: TSMC manufactures 90% of the world's advanced chips (sub-7nm). Every AI model, every hypersonic missile guidance system, and every autonomous drone fleet depends on a single facility in Taiwan — a geographical concentration of strategic vulnerability that has no historical precedent. The CHIPS Act ($52 billion in US subsidies) and equivalent programs in Japan, South Korea, and the EU are attempts to diversify this dependency, but the lead times are measured in decades, not quarters.

Second, the intelligence infrastructure layer: AI compute is becoming a national security asset class. The US export controls on advanced GPUs to China are not trade policy — they are an admission that compute capability has joined nuclear weapons and aircraft carriers as a determinant of geopolitical power. The nations and corporations that control the hardware layer will control the intelligence layer.

Third, the financialization layer: NVDA has become the single-stock proxy for this entire thesis, with a market capitalization exceeding the combined GDP of most G20 nations. This concentration risk is itself a source of contradiction — the market is pricing NVDA as if the AI revolution benefits one company, when the structural reality is that compute hegemony reshapes entire economies.

The GAP on this narrative spikes when media covers every NVDA earnings call as a consumer tech story, while CFTC positioning and semiconductor ETF flows show institutional capital treating the chip supply chain as a sovereign security asset.
