## Structural Thesis

China's economic trajectory is not a "growth story" or a "collapse thesis" — it is the construction of a parallel Eurasian capital architecture that will compete with, and in some domains replace, the Western-dominated Bretton Woods system over the next two decades.

The Belt and Road Initiative, despite its well-documented debt sustainability problems, has physically connected 140+ countries to Chinese infrastructure standards, Chinese industrial equipment, and Chinese development financing. This is not charity — it is the physical layer of a new capital architecture. When a BRI-funded port in East Africa runs on Huawei 5G infrastructure, financed by China Exim Bank, with commodities priced in RMB futures, the dollar is irrelevant to that transaction chain.

The BRICS expansion (Iran, Egypt, Ethiopia, UAE, Saudi Arabia) and the New Development Bank represent the institutional layer. These are not rhetorical groupings — they are trade-weighted coalitions of commodity producers and industrial consumers constructing settlement mechanisms that bypass SWIFT and the dollar clearing system. The media covers BRICS as a geopolitical talking shop. The capital flows show bilateral trade settlement in RMB growing at 35% annually.

The domestic challenges — property sector overhang, demographic decline, regulatory opacity — are real and suppress headline valuations (FXI trades at a structural discount). But the capital architecture being built externally operates on a different timeline and a different risk calculus. The GAP spikes when media fixates on Chinese economic weakness while institutional capital quietly positions in the infrastructure assets that will define the Eurasian trade corridor for the next 50 years.
